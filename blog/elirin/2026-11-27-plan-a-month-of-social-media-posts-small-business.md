# How to Plan a Month of Social Media Posts for Your Small Business (Without the Stress)

Most small business owners know they should post on Facebook and Instagram regularly. The hard part is not the posting itself. It is deciding *what* to post, every few days, while also running the business. The result is usually a burst of posts, then weeks of silence.

A monthly content plan fixes that. You make the big decisions once, in one sitting, and the rest of the month becomes simple execution. Here is a practical way to do it.

## 1. Start with one goal for the month

Before you think about posts, decide what you want this month's social media to do. Pick one main goal, for example:

- Bring more people to a seasonal offer or event
- Get more bookings, calls or messages
- Grow awareness in your local area
- Remind existing customers that you exist and keep them coming back

One clear goal keeps your posts focused. If every post tries to do everything, none of them does anything well.

## 2. Choose three or four content themes

Themes are the recurring "types" of post you rotate through. They stop you from staring at a blank screen. A good mix for most small businesses:

- **Helpful tips:** short advice related to what you sell (how to choose, how to care for, common mistakes).
- **Behind the scenes:** your team, your workspace, how things are made or prepared.
- **Proof:** finished work, before-and-after photos, happy customers (with their permission).
- **Offers and calls to action:** what you want people to do next, such as book, visit or message you.

Aim for most posts to be helpful or interesting, and only some to be direct offers. People follow accounts that give them something, not accounts that only sell.

## 3. Decide how often you can realistically post

Consistency matters more than volume. Three good posts a week, every week, beat a daily schedule that collapses after ten days. Look honestly at your time and pick a rhythm you can keep:

- **Light:** 2 posts per week
- **Steady:** 3 to 4 posts per week
- **Active:** 5 or more posts per week, plus Stories

Write the days down. For example: Monday tip, Wednesday behind the scenes, Friday offer.

## 4. Fill in the calendar in one sitting

Now open a simple calendar (a spreadsheet works fine) and give every posting day a theme and a one-line idea. Do the whole month at once. It is much faster than deciding post by post.

A useful format for each row:

| Date | Theme | Idea | Call to action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Tip | 3 mistakes customers make when… | Save this post |
| Wed | Behind the scenes | Morning prep at the shop | Visit us this week |
| Fri | Offer | Weekend special | Message us to book |

Also mark any dates that matter to your customers: holidays, local events, paydays, seasonal changes. Those are natural hooks for posts.

## 5. Create content in batches

Instead of making one post at a time, batch similar work together:

- Take all the photos you need in one session.
- Write all the captions for the week (or month) in one go.
- Keep your brand voice consistent: the same tone, the same way of greeting people, the same sign-off.

Batching turns social media from a daily interruption into a block of focused work once or twice a month.

## 6. Review before anything goes live

Give every post a quick check before it is published:

- Is the spelling and the price or date correct?
- Is the call to action clear?
- Does it sound like your business?
- If someone else helped create it, have you approved it?

A short review step prevents the most common and embarrassing mistakes.

## 7. Schedule, then look at the results

Schedule your posts ahead of time so they go out even on your busiest days. At the end of the month, look at what worked: which themes got the most reactions, comments, messages or clicks. Do more of that next month, and drop what did not land.

Over a few months, your plan gets sharper because it is based on what *your* audience actually responds to.
