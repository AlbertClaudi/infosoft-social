# Construction Time Tracking: Why Digital Clock In / Clock Out Beats Paper Timesheets

On many job sites, hours are still tracked on paper. A foreman fills in a sheet at the end of the week, someone types it up, and payroll waits until every sheet comes back. It works, until a sheet goes missing or nobody remembers exactly when a shift started.

## The hidden cost of paper timesheets

- **Guesswork**: hours written down days later are estimates, not records.
- **Double handling**: the same numbers are written, collected, and then typed again.
- **Delays**: payroll and job costing wait for the paperwork.
- **Disputes**: without a clear record, it is hard to settle a question about hours.

## What a digital clock in / clock out routine looks like

1. Each employee **clocks in** when the shift starts.
2. They **clock out** when it ends.
3. The hours are recorded straight away, in one place.
4. The office sees the hours without chasing paper.

It is a small habit for the crew, and it removes a lot of admin for the owner.

## Tips for rolling it out to your crew

- **Keep it simple**: clock in at the start, clock out at the end. No extra steps.
- **Explain the why**: accurate hours protect workers as much as the business.
- **Start with one crew**: let a team get used to it, then roll it out to everyone.
- **Check weekly**: review the hours each week so mistakes get fixed early.
